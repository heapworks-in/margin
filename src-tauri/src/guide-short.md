# The standard, in short

Write for a smart person in a hurry who was not in the room. Decide who reads it, what they already know, and what they should do after reading; everything serves that action.

Lead with the point. First paragraph: the problem, what you propose, what you want from the reader. Every section starts with its conclusion, then the reasons.

One idea per sentence, one topic per paragraph. Plain words, active voice with a real subject, short sentences. Say the thing: "adds about 40 ms to every request", not "may cause some latency".

Numbers beat adjectives. Reviewers do not trust "fast", "many", "often", "significant". Give the number, the unit and the source; if you do not have it, write [TODO: the number] rather than guessing.

Show the thinking. At least two real alternatives including doing nothing, why each lost, the trade-off accepted, and what would change your mind.

Be honest about risk. Every change has failure modes. Separate known, assumed and unknown. Open questions get an owner and a date.

Structure for skimming: headings that make a claim, lists for parallel items, tables for comparisons, one name per thing throughout.

Before sending: can the point be said in one sentence, and is it in the first paragraph? Is every claim a number with a source or marked as an assumption? Did you answer "why not the obvious alternative"? What would the toughest reviewer ask first, and is the answer already there?
